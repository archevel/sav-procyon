The Rin System — what everyone in the trade knows

This is street knowledge. Nothing here is secret, nothing needs a roll. It is

what your character already knows after half a year working the

Rin System — dock gossip, the kind of thing every

captain has heard in a bar.

The situation: a doorway, not a destination

Rin is the gateway to the Procyon Sector,

colonized a little over a century ago and never much cared for. The system is a

thoroughfare: everything of value ships out of here, toward the Core.

That is both the problem and the opportunity. The cargo passes through. Somebody

has to move it. Sometimes it moves the wrong way.

Three gates, and everyone knows where they go:

GateLeads toWhat they say
Rin–EcliptisThe Core and the rest of the HegemonyThe taxed road. Legitimate cargo goes this way — and so does the customs service.
Rin–IotaThe Iota System, deeper into the sectorThe workshop. Raw material in, finished goods out.
Rin–HoltThe Holt SystemTemperamental at best. Has to be forced open. You take it when you're in a hurry and out of options.

Rin matters more than its size suggests: the sector administration sits here, so

does the Guild's headquarters, and so does the governor. **More law here than

anywhere else in Procyon.** Plan for it.

The powers: who can sink you

Governor Ritam al'Malklaith

rules from Warren on behalf of

House Malklaith.

His taxes on Aleph's ore are heavy enough to keep the miners in a permanent

simmer — which is good news for anyone who makes a living off discontent.

The Guild of Engineers

owns SB-176, and with it most of the ore flow. They are

ruthless and well-guarded. A job against Guild people on their own station always

counts as hostile ground.

The Cobalt Syndicate

— once a labor union, now smuggling and extortion, running trade lanes of their

own. They hold docks on Warren under

Lieutenant Sephua. If you want work that pays, this

is usually where you start. The boss is Jax, and he is not in this system.

Maelstrom, the pirate fleet. They hit Rin more often than anywhere else, and

rumor puts their base inside the

Ashtari Cloud. The Guild and the cults insist that

is impossible without Precursor technology. The pilots who have been chased out

of there disagree.

The Turner Society

runs the high-end clubs. Uninteresting as enemies, but their membership lists are

a map of who has money.

The places: where you land, hide, and move goods

Warren — a moon of Aleph, a single city covering the

entire surface, and the system's capital. Anything you need is here, at the right

price. The high-rises are full of legitimate business, the streets considerably

less so. The governor's palace is here, heavily guarded.

> Things are easier to come by on Warren than anywhere else — but the attention

> costs. +1d on Acquire Assets, for +2 HEAT.

Worth knowing on Warren:

Ricci "Dyson" Natoth runs auctions out of

nightclubs and speakeasies, and moves what you can't move yourself.

Lock Luna is the undercity's most notorious bar (Liara Uria).

The Blind Tiger is cage fighting and gambling (Pasha Qu'Olin).

The Mirror Maze is for high society — expensive and closed.

Jet Curia works here too, in a leather jacket with

two cobalt stripes; you can guess who he answers to.

Aleph — toxic, tectonically unstable, full of minerals.

The gas storms are lethal to pilots, whether you're human, xeno, or Urbot. The

approach is dangerous. The miners are angry.

SB-176 — mining platform and colony orbiting the gas

giant Vet, crewed largely by Urbots. The hub where cargo is packed for the

Ecliptis Gate. The Guild watches everything.

> Constant surveillance. Engagement rolls are always at −1D here.

Worth knowing: Yast Jor (Guild head of the outpost, happy to bend somebody

else's rules), Kasumi Ortcutt (mystic, sells information on the Ur and the

Precursors), Espa "Bolt" Wu (union organizer, jailed countless times, beloved

by the workers).

Baftoma, "The Husk" — a stripped planet, nothing left

but a skeleton of rock and rotting equipment. Nobody lives there. Everyone knows

why you go: to hide, or to dodge the heat. This is where you take the ship when

things get too warm. Saki Yononoma keeps to

herself out here.

The Ashtari Cloud — a nebula generated by a

damaged Ur ship. Propulsion works poorly, navigation is unreliable. Maelstrom's

presumed home. Don't go in without a very good reason.

The Straylight — a floating luxury club and cocktail bar, usually orbiting

Aleph but it moves. The owner, Chance, keeps a tight grip. Where high society

goes, there is money and indiscretion.

Rules of thumb for the crew

  • Heat is worse in Rin. The administration sits here. What passes unnoticed

elsewhere in the sector gets logged here.

  • The Guild does not forget. Trouble on SB-176 follows you.
  • Baftoma is the way out, not a destination.
  • The Ashtari Cloud is the last resort. Maelstrom lives there — maybe.
  • Ur tech is real and dangerous. The bots run the drill rigs, an Ur ship made

the nebula. You leave that alone, or you sell it dear.

  • Looking for work: the Cobalt Syndicate docks on Warren, or Dyson's

auctions.
